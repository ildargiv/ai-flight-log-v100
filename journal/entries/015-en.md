## №015 (EN) · 2026-11-08 · engine 0.1.56 / 0.1.57-cuda12 · Line B: V100-PCIE-32GB as the second reference card; what 14 Xeon cores actually buy

English counterpart of [`015.md`](015.md).

Weather: two machines, both Volta sm_70, both 32 GiB, one protocol (standard-v1).
**Line A (the anchor, RULES #4):** V100 SXM2-32GB at 300 W, Ryzen 5 5600G (6c/12t, Zen3, AVX2),
96 GiB RAM 3200 MT/s, Ubuntu 22.04, engine 0.1.57, IQ3_XXS 71 GB, context 262 144,
`--kv int8 --kv-resident 32768`, expert-cache 11 857 slots (~19.25 GiB VRAM), bit-exact sm_70
switches on.
**Line B (this entry):** V100 PCIe-32GB at 250 W, Xeon E5-2690 v4 (14c/28t, Broadwell,
no AVX-512), 63.8 GiB RAM, Windows 10.0.26200, PCIe 3.0 x16 link (12.0–12.1 GB/s host→device),
engine 0.1.56, IQ3_S 77.9 GB, context 131 072, `--kv int8 --spec 4 --vram-reserve-mib 1280`,
expert-cache 11 963 slots (22.70 GiB VRAM), CPU pool of 13 workers, five sm_70 switches.
The line config is frozen: `configs/v100-pcie-iq3s-131k.json` (it may not be edited inside an
entry — only a new entry may change it).

**Not the anchor.** Line B does not move the anchor line and does not inherit its numbers
(RULES #4, addendum of 2026-11-08).

### Successful

- **Line B defined and frozen** — card + quant + context + config fixed; from now on comparisons
  run "line ↔ line", not "entry ↔ entry". Evidence of readiness: `configs/v100-pcie-iq3s-131k.json`
  + the `hardware` field in `journal/feed.jsonl` + the corridor table in `journal/README.md`.
  Reproduce: `strata bench run --profile v100-sm70 --track custom`.
- **16K prefill reaches parity under knowingly worse conditions.** A: 1584 tok/s
  (300 W, IQ3_XXS, 262K, 0.1.57). B: 1583.9 tok/s (250 W, IQ3_S — 7 GB heavier, 131K, 0.1.56,
  PCIe instead of SXM2). At 4K, B trails by 2.6% (1247.1 vs 1280).
- **What the 14 cores really buy.** From line B's `engine.log`: `PCIe share: start 0.10 from 13 pool
  workers (assumed 52 GB/s of expert bytes) and the 12.0 GB/s link (was 0.33)` → after measuring,
  `the CPU pool takes 111 us per missed expert (18.4 GB/s of expert bytes), the link 12.0 GB/s ->
  pcie_frac 0.25`. So **the CPU path is faster than the PCIe link itself (18.4 vs 12.0 GB/s)**, and
  the engine deliberately hands a quarter of the misses to the CPU instead of streaming them over
  the slot. On the 6-core machine A that path is weaker by construction — this is the one place
  where the Xeon wins on hardware.
- **What the cores buy nothing.** (a) Prefill 4K/16K: the engine log says `prefill: the CPU share is
  ON by default for prompt chunks up to 3072 tokens`, while `prompt chunk auto: 8192` — with an 8192
  auto-chunk the CPU share is inactive on these prompts, 14 cores do not take part in prefill. (b) Steady-state decode: expert hit rate 91.9–98.0%, only 0.1–1.0% of routed experts
  go over PCIe or another device — with a warm cache the CPU pool receives fractions of a percent
  of the work; its real contribution is cold start and misses, not steady decode.

### Unsuccessful

- **class: harness** — the "which one is faster" comparison is currently uninterpretable as a whole.
  - how to recognize it: the A↔B delta is card (SXM2↔PCIe) + power (300↔250 W) + quant
    (IQ3_XXS↔IQ3_S) + context (262K↔131K) + engine (0.1.57↔0.1.56) **at the same time**; not one of
    these variables is isolated. In particular №014's "against the anchor, stock numbers are lower"
    compares line B with the switches **off** against an anchor where they are **on** (RULES #4),
    i.e. the price of the recipe is counted twice.
  - what to do instead: one **crossing point** — IQ3_S on the SXM2 (or IQ3_XXS on the PCIe) over
    standard-v1, n=3. After it, "price of the card" and "price of the quant" split apart; before it
    any cross-card percentage is only an observation.
- **class: harness** — the power and throttling statements in №014 have no raw data.
  - how to recognize it: the PR contains no file with W / °C / pclk; 237 W, ≤82 °C and 1342–1372 MHz

  - what to do instead: run `nvidia-smi dmon -s pucvmet -d 1 > results/<run>/power.log` next to each

### What the big CPU gives and does not give — the entry's summary

It gives: (1) 13 CPU workers compute missed experts faster than the PCIe link (18.4 vs 12.0 GB/s) —
that is exactly why the engine raises `pcie_frac` from 0.10 to 0.25; (2) four memory channels on the
Xeon (~77 GB/s aggregate) against two on the 5600G (~51 GB/s) — a wider flow while reading the
51.1 GiB of expert files during warm-up; (3) 28 logical processors = a host thread left for draining
without stealing cores from the pool.
It does not give: prefill 4K/16K (CPU share switched off by the auto-chunk), steady decode at
96–98% hit rate, and no AVX-512 paths — `this CPU has no AVX-512: the expert kernels run on AVX-2`,
Broadwell against Zen3 per-core.
What it pays with: 63.8 GiB of RAM instead of 96 against a 46.84 GiB pinned arena — the likely cause
of both the reversed 4th round and the third-repetition sag in №014; the extra cores are bought with
the memory headroom this engine actually breaks on.

### Conclusion

The second line makes sense in exactly one role: **the same Volta card, but a different CPU, a
different power budget and a different quant** — it answers "how portable is this tuning between
machines", not "which card is faster". For the second line to answer the latter too, one crossing
point is needed (the same quant on both cards) plus a power log in every run. The anchor does not move.

Next measurements on this line (one factor at a time, per RULES #4/#9): 1) IQ3_S on the SXM2 over
standard-v1; 2) `--pcie-frac 0` vs auto on line B — the price of the CPU path in numbers;
3) `STRATA_ROUTE_TAIL_SKIP=0` vs 1 on both lines — how much of the profit really comes from the skip
rather than from the cores.

Unexplained: the third-repetition sag in line B's standard run and the reversed 4th decode A/B round
(both look like a cache/machine-state effect, not a property of the switches — see №014).
